Data Card: Elliptic Bitcoin Dataset

BSFS x BSML · Graph ML for Money Laundering · Role 1 – Data & Exploration

1. Overview

  • Source: Elliptic, via Kaggle (ellipticco/elliptic-data-set)
  • Paper: Weber et al. (2019), Anti-Money Laundering in Bitcoin: Experimenting with Graph Convolutional Networks for Financial Forensics, arXiv:1908.02591
  • Task: classify Bitcoin transactions as illicit or licit
  • One row = one Bitcoin transaction

6. How transactions connect (graph structure)

  • 100.0% of edges connect transactions in the same time step, so no edge crosses the train/test boundary
  • 49 connected groups (components); the largest has 7,880 transactions
  • Average connections per transaction: illicit 2.01 · licit 3.10 · unknown 2.10

7. Key observations

  • Illicit transactions are rare: 9.8% of labelled ones. Don't judge models on plain accuracy; use illicit-class F1, precision/recall or PR-AUC.
  • The time step with the most illicit transactions is 32.
  • Per the paper, a dark-market shutdown around time step 43 changes illicit behaviour, so models may struggle in the late test period.
  • Illicit activity comes in bursts, not a steady flow. The count jumps from under 20 to around 250–340 between neighbouring time steps (e.g. step 9 ≈ 250 → step 10 ≈ 20; step 32 ≈ 340 → step 33 ≈ 25). This suggests criminal activity happens in waves or campaigns.
  • The first 6 time steps contain almost no illicit activity (under 2% of labelled transactions), even though step 1 is the busiest time step overall (~7,900 transactions).
  • The test period looks different from the training period. Illicit share drops from 11.6% in train to 6.5% in test, and right after the dark-market shutdown (steps 43–46) it falls close to 0% before recovering to ~12% by step 49. Models trained on steps 1–34 will face behaviour they never saw.
  • Illicit transactions are less connected than licit ones: on average 2.01 connections vs 3.10 for licit, about a third fewer.
  • Neighbours of illicit transactions are often unlabelled. In the example network shown, the illicit transaction receives money from 3 transactions and every neighbour is unknown. Graph models therefore need to learn from the network structure, not only from neighbours' labels.

8. Caveats

  • 77% of transactions are unlabelled: keep them in the graph, but don't train or score on them
  • Features are anonymised, so they can't be interpreted by name
  • Train and test periods may behave differently (see the dark-market point above)
